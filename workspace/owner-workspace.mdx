The Owner workspace is for the person responsible for the business as a whole.

Its job is not to duplicate Sales, Accounting, Inventory, or Support. It is to surface the decisions and cross-functional information an owner needs without forcing that person to become the owner of every Lead, Invoice, Purchase Order, and Ticket.

## What an owner should be able to answer

A useful owner review answers:

- What needs attention today?
- Which customer or sales work is stuck?
- What money is due, overdue, or waiting for action?
- Is stock creating a problem?
- Is customer support at risk?
- What is my team working on?
- Which approvals need a decision?
- Are there access, data, or system issues I should know about?
- How is the business performing compared with the period I care about?

## Overview and reviews

Use the overview for broad status and shortcuts. Reviews and approvals are for items waiting for authorized attention.

Resolve the source record after making the decision. An approved Bill still belongs in Payables. An access decision still belongs to the access model. The Owner workspace should point you to the work, not become a second copy of it.

## Targets

Owner surfaces can include sales and service target views. Use these to review whether the business or team is moving toward expected outcomes without opening every Deal or Ticket individually.

## Finance

Owner shortcuts can connect into payables and financial reports so the owner can review obligations and financial position while finance users continue to own day-to-day accounting work.

## Members and access

Owners can review members, Teams, roles, product-area access, and permission controls. This is where a small business can keep one shared system without giving every person the same access.

Use [Run Lynka with a small team](/teams/run-small-team) before copying owner access to ordinary users.

## System and data attention

Workspace health belongs at the owner level when the problem crosses product areas or affects the reliability of the business system.

Use attention signals to find the issue, then correct the underlying record, integration, permission, or process.

## Automation should reduce owner chasing

A strong small-business system should not require the owner to ask every employee whether they followed up a Lead, whether a Ticket has an assignee, or whether an Invoice is overdue.

Lynka's automation catalog includes attention rules for these kinds of conditions. Use automation to surface exceptions and keep responsibility with the person who owns the work.
