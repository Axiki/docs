The **General Ledger** is the detailed history behind your account balances.

If the Trial Balance tells you *what an account balance is*, the General Ledger helps explain *how it got there*.

## What you use it for

Open the General Ledger when:

- a Balance Sheet account looks too high or too low
- Profit & Loss contains an unexpected expense or revenue amount
- Accounts Receivable or Accounts Payable does not reconcile as expected
- cash or bank balances need investigation
- you want to trace a Journal correction
- you are reviewing activity before period close

## How the ledger relates to Journals

Operational records such as posted Invoices, supplier Bills, Payments, cash transactions, or explicit Journal Entries can create or relate to accounting entries according to the workspace's posting rules.

The General Ledger groups the posted debit and credit activity by account so you can see the sequence of changes.

## Investigate one account at a time

Start with the account that explains the problem. For example, if Accounts Receivable looks wrong, do not scan every account in the chart.

Filter the relevant period and account, then look for:

- unexpected large entries
- duplicate-looking postings
- reversals or corrections
- entries outside the expected date range
- unusual counterpart accounts

## Source documents and the ledger are not the same thing

An Invoice total can include tax while the revenue account contains the net revenue portion. A customer Payment can affect cash and Accounts Receivable without changing revenue again.

That is why an operational document total should not always equal one General Ledger line.

## Do not silently rewrite posted history

If an old posted entry is wrong, use the supported reversal, correction, or adjustment workflow when possible. Rewriting a settled historical source can make today's report look cleaner while damaging the audit trail needed to explain what happened.

## Example: revenue looks too high

A practical check is:

1. open Profit & Loss and identify the revenue account or period that looks wrong
2. open the General Ledger for the corresponding revenue account
3. inspect the entries behind the increase
4. open the related Journal or commercial source
5. confirm whether tax, duplicate posting, date, or classification explains the difference
6. correct the source through the appropriate accounting control

## Use it with other reports

The General Ledger is investigative. The Trial Balance is structural. Profit & Loss and the Balance Sheet are presentation reports. Cash Flow explains movement in cash. Aging reports focus on outstanding customer or supplier balances.

Each report is strongest when used for the question it was designed to answer.
