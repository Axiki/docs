Posting is the bridge between the customer invoice and the accounting ledger. It turns the commercial billing document into the Accounts Receivable, revenue, tax, and other accounting consequences defined by the posting logic.

## Posting is not the same as sending

You can prepare or send an invoice without treating it as paid. Posting also does not mean cash was received. It records the accounting obligation created by billing the customer.

## Access required

Posting is controlled by both plan capability and permission. The workspace entitlement must allow invoice posting, and the user needs `invoice.manage` for the posting action.

Invoice posting is not included during the 15-day free access. Pro and Teams enable Accounting and invoice posting when the workspace and user access allow it; there is no ongoing free plan.

## What the accounting entry represents

A typical posted customer invoice can affect:

- Accounts Receivable for the amount the customer owes
- revenue for the net revenue portion
- tax payable for tax collected on behalf of the tax authority
- other configured accounts where the invoice structure requires them

The exact journal depends on the invoice and accounting configuration.

## Billings versus revenue

Invoice totals can include tax. Revenue reporting can use the revenue or subtotal amount. Therefore the Agreements billings trend does not have to equal Sales Revenue in the ledger.

That difference is expected when the two metrics answer different questions.

## Posting problems

Lynka includes **Invoice accounting posting incomplete** in the automation catalog. It looks for issued or settled invoices missing required accounting links.

If posting fails, do not mark the invoice paid or create a manual revenue journal just to make the dashboard look right. Resolve the posting issue so the source invoice and ledger remain connected.
