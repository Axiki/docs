Lynka is built for small teams as well as solo owners.

A small team already has real coordination problems: one person owns a Lead, another handles the Quote, finance records the Payment, operations receives stock, and the owner wants to understand the whole business without doing every task personally.

Teams is the layer that makes those responsibilities shareable without turning every user into an administrator.

## Teams is more than extra logins

Lynka separates six concepts that are easy to confuse:

**Membership** answers whether the person belongs to the workspace.

**Product area access** answers whether they can open Sales, Agreements, Inventory, Accounting, Support, Marketing, LeadGen, or Reports.

**Action permissions** answer what they can do, such as assigning a Lead, posting an Invoice, reversing a Journal, or managing Teams.

**Record scope** answers which records they can see, such as Mine, a team, or wider workspace data where allowed.

**Ownership** answers who is responsible for a specific record.

**Team membership** groups people so the business can organize collaboration and team-level scope.

These layers let a five-person business stay simple without becoming careless about customer and financial data.

## A practical small-team model

An owner can keep broad visibility. Sales users can work Leads, Deals, Activities, and commercial documents. Finance can work Agreements and Accounting. Operations can work Inventory. Support can work Tickets.

One person can also wear two hats and belong to more than one team.

## Role defaults are a starting point

The live role model includes defaults for Owner, Admin, Finance, Marketing, Support, Sales, Rep, and Member patterns.

Do not treat the role name as the final access decision. Plan capabilities, enabled product areas, user overrides, action permissions, and record scope still apply.

## Managers do not need Admin

A manager can receive team-level visibility and the actions needed to supervise work without receiving every workspace or finance permission.

[Manager visibility](/teams/manager-visibility) shows how to widen review while keeping sensitive controls separate.

## Ownership keeps work from disappearing

Assign Leads, Deals, Tickets, and other supported records to the person expected to act next. Team access can let several people see the record, but somebody should still own the responsibility.

Lynka's attention automation includes rules for unowned Leads and unassigned Tickets because shared access without responsibility can still produce missed work.

## Approvals add control where it matters

Use approvals when a second decision is worth the delay. They are a collaboration control, not a requirement to make every small action bureaucratic.

## When someone leaves

Review their open work, reassign responsibility, update team memberships, and only then remove workspace access. Historical activity can remain part of the business record after the person is no longer active.
